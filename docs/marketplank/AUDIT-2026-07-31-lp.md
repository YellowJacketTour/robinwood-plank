# Audit — MarketplankVault V2 LP accounting (2026-07-31)

**Scope:** the V1 → V2 delta only, plus its interaction with the AMM.
**Question asked:** is V2 safe to migrate V1 holders into?
**Answer: no. Do not migrate users into V2.**

The finding below is reproducible on a local chain:

```
npx hardhat test test/contracts/VaultLp.audit.test.ts
```

## What V2 changed

Commit `1a856ac` is the entire difference between the deployed V1 and the
deployed V2: **+84 / −3 lines, one file**. It adds `lpShareCredit` /
`lpEthCredit`, `contributeLiquidity(uint256)`, `removeLiquidity(uint256,uint256)`,
two events and two errors. Deposit, redeem, randomness, the AMM and the
solvency invariant are byte-for-byte identical.

Those two functions had **zero Solidity coverage** before this audit — the
still-open CAP-E6 item.

## LP-1 — CRITICAL — contribute → trade → remove extracts the pool

`contributeLiquidity` (`contracts/MarketplankVault.sol:561-573`) accepts a
**one-sided** contribution: line 563 rejects only the case where *both* sides
are zero. A one-sided contribution moves the constant-product price.

`removeLiquidity` (`:589-608`) returns the **nominal** credited amount. It is
capped by the caller's own credit (594, 600) and by the live reserve (595,
601), and by nothing else — not pro-rata, not price-aware, no `k` check.

So a contributor is insulated from a price move they themselves caused, while
everyone else in the pool absorbs it. Sandwiching a trade between the two calls
converts that asymmetry into cash.

### Closed form

Against reserves `E` (ETH) and `S` (shares), an attacker holding `s` shares
takes the **entire** ETH reserve by contributing

> **`e = E · S / s`**

Then: `contributeLiquidity{value: e}(0)` → `sellShares(s, 0)` → `removeLiquidity(0, e)`.

### Executed, not theorised

`test/contracts/VaultLp.audit.test.ts`, on a 4 ETH / 3 share pool with the
attacker holding one share:

```
pool before      : 4.0 ETH / 3.0 shares
attacker spends  : 12.0 ETH contributed (+ 1 share)
attacker receives: 16.0 ETH
NET PROFIT       : 4.0 ETH  (a fair sale of that share = 1.0)
pool ETH after   : 0.0 ETH
```

Four ETH of profit on a share worth one ETH, and the pool's ETH reserve is
zero afterwards.

### No closed form needed — and it is flash-loanable

The optimal size above is not a requirement. Whenever the sale exceeds the
*original* reserve `E₀`, withdrawing `min(credit, reserve)` yields

> **profit = exactly `E₀`, and the reserve ends at zero**

for *any* contribution size. Algebraically, if `ethOut > E₀` then
`ethOut + (E₀ + D − ethOut) − D = E₀` regardless of `D`. Measured with a
deliberately non-optimal 20 ETH contribution against the same 4 ETH pool
(the closed form would have said 12):

```
contribution     : 20.0 ETH (arbitrary, fully recycled)
shares given up  : 1.0 (fair value 1.0 ETH)
NET PROFIT       : 4.0 ETH
pool ETH after   : 0.0 ETH
```

The contribution is returned within the same transaction, so **the attacker
needs no capital of their own — this is flash-loanable, and costs only gas.**

The one precondition is a share position large enough that the sale exceeds
`E₀`. Against a thin pool that is trivial: on the live V2 reserves, ~0.08
shares and a 1 ETH flash loan suffice.

### Share-side mirror

`contributeLiquidity(σ)` → `buyShares{value: v}` → `removeLiquidity(σ, 0)` with
`v = S · E / σ` empties the share reserve instead. Measured:

```
attacker spends    : 12.0 ETH (+ 1.0 share contributed and returned)
shares obtained    : 4.0   (an honest buy of 12.0 ETH = 2.25)
pool shares after  : 0.0
```

Those shares redeem 1:1 for NFTs, so this variant converts to **real
inventory**, not just pool depth.

### Why the existing defences do not fire

- **`nonReentrant` is irrelevant.** The three calls are sequential, not nested.
  `MockLpDrainer` performs all three in a single transaction, so the sequence is
  atomic and riskless — not an arbitrage that can be raced or front-run.
- **The solvency invariant stays green.** `_assertSolvent()` (`:819`) relates
  `totalSupply()` to held NFTs. Neither LP function touches either. The vault is
  fully solvent in share terms while its ETH is being taken, which is exactly
  why `VaultSolvency.fuzz.test.ts` never caught this.
- **FINDING 1's guards never trip.** `buyShares`/`sellShares` revert on an empty
  reserve (508, 533); both reserves are non-zero at every step of the attack.

### The fee objection, tested

> "A trade sandwiched in the middle pays LP fees."

**There are no LP or swap fees in this contract.** Lines 495–608 contain no fee
or bps term whatsoever; `buyShares:511` and `sellShares:536` are bare
constant-product. Every fee in the file is at `:335` (deposit), `:367` (random
redeem) and `:479-480` (targeted redeem) — all on the NFT⇄share boundary, which
the attack never crosses.

Both acquisition paths were measured:

- **Attacker deposits an NFT** and forfeits the 1% mint fee: the fee is worth
  0.0000613 ETH against live reserves; the extraction nets 0.0209 ETH. **341×.**
- **Attacker buys the share on the AMM**: 0.008674709 ETH, no fee charged at
  all, same 0.0209 ETH profit.

**On the fee's denomination.** The mint fee is charged in **shares**, not ETH
(`:335`, `fee = SHARE_UNIT * mintFeeBps / BPS_DENOMINATOR`, minted to the
treasury). Priced at the pool rate, 0.01 shares is 0.0000613 ETH. For that
share-denominated cost to cancel an ETH-denominated extraction of 0.0209 ETH, a
share would have to be worth **2.09 ETH — 341× the pool-implied price** of
0.006130954. Even at a generous 1 ETH per share the attack still nets 2.1×
the fee.

The denomination is therefore not load-bearing, and two independent reasons
make it irrelevant:

1. **The fee is avoidable.** `buyShares` charges nothing, so the attacker
   acquires shares without ever touching `deposit`.
2. **Profit does not depend on the share count.** In the full-drain case profit
   is exactly `E₀` regardless of `s`, provided `s` is large enough to clear the
   threshold. Shaving 1% (or 10%) off `s` raises the flash-loaned capital
   slightly and changes the profit not at all.

Measured at the constructor's **maximum permitted fee** (1000 bps, `:308`),
with the attacker buying shares on the fee-free path:

```
mint fee         : 1000 bps (contract maximum), charged in shares
shares acquired  : 1.0 via fee-free buyShares
pool ETH before  : 6.0
attacker ETH gain: 6.0
pool ETH after   : 0.0
```

The entire reserve, at the highest fee the contract can be deployed with.

Granting the premise anyway and simulating a hypothetical swap fee: at 30 bps
the attacker still nets 0.020819022; at 300 bps, 0.020020295. **A swap fee does
not fix this.** It is a percentage haircut on a payout that scales with the
attacker's own contribution — they simply size up.

### Root cause

V2 tracks LP positions as **absolute credits** where Uniswap V2 and NFTX use
**proportional LP tokens**. The NFTX comparison is right as *intent*; the
divergence is the accounting model. Under proportional accounting the same
sequence returns the attacker a pro-rata slice of *current* reserves, so the
price move is absorbed by their own position and the profit collapses to
exactly the fair value of the share sold.

The comment at `:552-555` anticipates only the LP being short-changed
("removal then fails closed until reserves recover"). It fails *open* whenever
the reserve covers the credit — which the attacker guarantees by sizing their
own contribution.

### Partial extraction is always available

The full drain needs a large enough share position. Below that threshold the
same sequence still returns a slippage improvement of **`s·e/(S+s)`** funded
out of the shared reserve, at any contribution size, risk-free. On the live
reserves with 0.1 shares and a 0.01 ETH contribution that is a **47.8%**
improvement over honest execution. There is no size at which the sequence is
not profitable.

## Independent confirmation

Two auditors reviewed the contract without being told what this audit had
found. Both independently identified the same root cause, both recommended
proportional LP accounting, and both returned a do-not-migrate verdict. One
contributed the sizing-free / flash-loanable variant above; the other
contributed the partial-extraction formula. Neither found any additional
critical issue outside the V1→V2 delta.

One correction made during verification: the sizing-free variant as first
stated ("any contribution size") is only true when the sale exceeds `E₀` *and*
the removal takes `min(credit, reserve)` rather than the full contribution.
Withdrawing the full contribution reverts `InsufficientLpReserve` once the
reserve has fallen below it. The corrected form is what
`MockLpDrainer.drainEthMax` implements and what the test above measures.

## Supporting findings

- **LP-2 (HIGH)** — credits are never marked down by trading losses, so a stale
  credit is a perpetual senior claim on any ETH that later arrives via
  `buyShares`. A later buyer's ETH funds an earlier LP's exit at par. The
  contract keeps no aggregate credit total, so it cannot detect this.
- **LP-3 (MEDIUM)** — removal may reduce either side to exactly zero; there is no
  `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY` floor. Post-`openPool`, seeding is dead (618, 642) and
  trading reverts `EmptyVault` (508, 533), so the only recovery is a fresh
  `contributeLiquidity` — which re-arms LP-1.
- **LP-4 (MEDIUM)** — credits are first-come-first-served against one shared
  reserve, so honest LPs lose a bank run and the UI's "removable" figure is
  routinely wrong.
- **LP-5 (informational)** — **not** a reentrancy bug. Line 604's ETH `call`
  follows strict CEI and is `nonReentrant`. Recorded so future readers stop
  looking here; the value leaves through the front door.

## Consequence for the migration

V2's *only* difference from V1 is the two flawed functions. V1 has no LP
surface and therefore no LP drain. **V2 is currently strictly worse than V1**,
so the planned V1 → V2 migration would move users toward the weaker vault.

Recommendation: **do not migrate to V2.** Deploy a V3 and migrate V1 → V3
directly, leaving V2 as a small second legacy (8 NFTs) rather than a populated
one.

## V3 scope

Blocker:

- **Proportional LP accounting** — Uniswap-V2-style units minted on ratio
  deposits, burned pro-rata, with a `MINIMUM_LIQUIDITY` floor. Fixes LP-1
  through LP-4 together.

Strongly worth doing while the file is open (each one skipped becomes a V4):

- **A swap fee (~30 bps to the reserve).** Not a mitigation for LP-1 — see
  above — but without it there is no economic reason to be an LP at all, and
  seeded ETH is a one-way subsidy to arbitrageurs. Open finding VAULT-4.
- **ETH-denominated mint/redeem fees**, ending the 0.99 / 1.01 asymmetry that
  strands every single-deposit holder with ~0.02 shares of dust. What keeps
  redemption from draining the vault is that each redeem burns exactly one unit
  of backing per NFT — the *share-denominated fee is not load-bearing for
  solvency*. Moving fees to ETH keeps that one-unit burn (`deposit` mints a full
  `1e18`, redeem burns exactly `1e18`), so the invariant gets strictly simpler,
  not more fragile. Note the treasury cannot presently redeem both of its own
  seeded V2 NFTs because of the 0.99/1.01 asymmetry — the ETH-fee model removes
  that too.
- **`depositMany` / `redeemTargetMany`** — migration is exactly this workload.
- **Aggregate LP totals + a `poolComposition()` view**, so the contract can
  self-diagnose LP-2 and the UI can state what is genuinely removable.
- **A `VAULT_VERSION` constant and explicit capability view**, replacing the
  deployed-bytecode selector sniffing at `lib/market/vault.ts:810-882`, which
  does not scale to a third vault.

Defer: a FIFO redeem queue. The single vault-wide slot is a real grief lever,
but every concurrency scheme reintroduces the draw steerability that two prior
audit rounds were spent eliminating. Price the grief with a refundable bond
instead.

Skip: relaxing targeted redeem of a pinned token (closed FINDING 6); any admin
withdraw, pause or proxy (CAP-S2 — the non-rug property is the vault's main
honest claim); redeploying `DrandBeacon` (reuse it — the drand parameter
cross-verification was the expensive part of the V2 launch, and a vault-only
V3 inherits it untouched).

## Test coverage added

- `test/contracts/VaultLp.audit.test.ts` — 12 tests: all three exploits, the neutral
  control, credit provenance, stale-credit seniority, the brick, gating and
  boundary exactness, orthogonality to a pending draw, and `ethReserve()` ≤ real
  balance. Closes CAP-E6.
- `contracts/test/MockLpDrainer.sol` — performs all three legs in one
  transaction, proving atomicity.
- `test/contracts/VaultSolvency.fuzz.test.ts` — LP operations added to the op
  selector, plus two new reporters: aggregate LP credit versus reserve, and
  constant-product `k` falling across a pure trade.

**Honest limitation:** random fuzzing does **not** find LP-1. The attack needs a
deliberately sized contribution (`e = E·S/s`); random amounts miss it. The
deterministic exploit test is the proof, and the two fuzz reporters are
regression guards to be flipped to hard assertions (`expect(...).to.equal(0n)`)
once LP accounting is proportional.
