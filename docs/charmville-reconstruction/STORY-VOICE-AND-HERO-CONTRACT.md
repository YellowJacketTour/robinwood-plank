# Story voice and hero contract

Approved creative direction, September 13, 2026. Narrative and implementation requirements, not shipped gameplay. Read alongside HOMETOWN-HOMESTEAD-AND-JUSTICE.md and STARTER-NARRATIVE-PLACEMENT.md.

## The premise

The hero is seven. Loving parents proudly announce that their work is complete: the child must now venture into the world and make a life. Their confidence is comically disproportionate to the child's experience. The departure begins a genuinely independent survival, discovery and friendship journey. Do not soften it into an afternoon errand. Home can remain emotionally meaningful without parents solving every problem or resetting the adventure.

Love is the everlasting force at the center of the story. It appears through care, loyalty, reconciliation, shared effort and companions who stay. Independence and vulnerability coexist. The hero can miss home, struggle, accept help and become capable. The user's "Spartan survival" and "child PTSD" language describes a demanding emotional coming-of-age direction; do not turn PTSD into a joke, a diagnosis assigned to every hero, or a claim that suffering is required for growth.

The humor uses original affectionate absurdity, precise language and a small unexpected turn. Adults and institutions may be ridiculous; the child's fear is not the punchline. Avoid copied quotations, relentless quips, cruelty presented as wisdom, and long exposition. Emotional moments may simply be sincere.

## Opening script draft

One entry is a short dialogue beat, not a promise it fits the current bitmap font without measurement.

1. Mother: "Seven already. We're so proud."
2. Father: "You're ready for the world. We've packed a sandwich."
3. Hero choice: "The whole world?" / "What kind of sandwich?"
4. Father, world response: "Start with the bit outside."
5. Mother, sandwich response: "Your favorite. Some traditions must survive independence."
6. Mother: "These Burning Heart seeds came from our first garden."
7. Mother: "They're yours now. Care for them. Share what grows."
8. Father: "Make something of yourself. Lunch counts."
9. Departure: "The gate is open. Your story starts here."

Let the child walk through the gate. Do not teleport them into a dangerous screen while a speech obscures it. The first garden, water, shelter and friendly guide must be reachable through authored, tested paths. The tutorial teaches actions with actual resources and outcomes; narration cannot grant a harvest or counterfeit a saved item.

## Learning through the journey

Begin with movement and observation, then seed, soil, water, harvest, satchel and sharing. Continue into food, animal care, tools, fishing, crafting, companion trust, safe combat and town exchange. Increase decisions as abilities grow; do not front-load a survival dashboard. Show one clear next action and let optional exploration breathe.

Failure teaches a recoverable consequence. Preserve essential tutorial tools and a route back to basic subsistence. Friendship helps through concrete mechanics such as assistance, healing, shared journeys and personal party loot. Do not imply affection has a guaranteed exchange price or that spending buys friendship.

Original environmental copy examples:

- Garden: "Water here. Encouragement elsewhere."
- Town road: "Town ahead. Bring yourself."
- Pond: "Fish live here. Appointments are unreliable."
- Exchange: "Check the price. Then check your pockets."
- Guard, surrender: "Put it back. We'll begin again."
- Companion reunion: "There you are."

Keep practical instructions separate from flavor where a joke could obscure the action, cost, risk or destination. Wanted escalation remains compatible with a warm world through deliberate crime inputs, proportional consequences and restitution. Innocent players and companions are not targets of comic cruelty.

## Equal starter presentation

Offer a girl hero with the same gameplay capability as the Link-based starter. A Zelda-inspired presentation is a candidate, not an assertion that a complete compatible sprite set exists. Avatar presentation must not alter speed, reach, collision, action timing, gear access, tutorial treatment or progression.

Before shipping a starter, audit every runtime action and directional frame: idle, walk, turn, sword slash and charge, shield reposition, damage, defeat/recovery, lift/carry/throw, bow, hammer, axe, pickaxe, rod, planting/watering, swimming and all supported special abilities. Include front/back equipment occlusion, attachment anchors, palette changes, shadow/feet position and crop/tree layers. Mark missing frames explicitly; a recolored idle sprite is not parity.

Use one shared action contract with presentation-specific frames and anchors. Preserve collision geometry independently of artwork. Verify equipped and unequipped variants in every supported direction, including camera zoom and followers. Starter selection must preview the actual available art and persist the chosen appearance without recreating the account's inventory or companions.

## Acceptance and continuity

- Family scene and departure play once, resume correctly, and never override a returning player's location.
- Both starter presentations complete the same tutorial with matching action coverage.
- Burning Heart is its own valid inventory/crop identity before this script replaces the current Oran tutorial.
- Dialogue supports measured wrapping, text-size settings, localization expansion, speaker identity, advance/back and repeatable instructions. Essential choices never depend on an expiring timer.
- Emotional and humorous text receives a human-readable contextual review alongside the actual scene, not only string validation.
- Child protagonist age is fictional characterization, not evidence of the account holder's age or permission for financial/community features.
- Current runtime remains the existing meadow/Oran opening until narrative, art, topology and authoritative progression are wired together. This document does not certify those changes.
