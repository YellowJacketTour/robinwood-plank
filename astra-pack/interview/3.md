Yes — a successful Stalk harvest returns **exactly one Stalk seed** plus the faces. Not two seeds. Not a chance of zero. The plant pays its own rent. The fruit is the surplus.

That is the grower’s seed system, not Zynga’s shop.

## What FarmVille actually did

FarmVille did **not** give the seed back. Every plant was a repurchase. You bought the seed from the market with coins, spent it into the plot, harvested **coins** (strawberries: 10 in, 35 out), and the seed was gone. Renewal was “profit in coins,” not “the plant remade itself.”

The thing that *looked* like seed-saving was the **bushel**: a random extra drop on harvest, stored in a stall, used for a two-hour mastery buff, a temporary license, or a capped neighbor take (3 bags per neighbor per day). Bushels were not required to replant. They were a social surplus on top of a commodity seed loop.

Trees and animals were the perennial exception: pay once, keep harvesting. That is the closest FarmVille got to permaculture, and it is why dairy/coops printed coins until the economy went cartoonish.

Alenda Chang’s reading in *ISLE* is the academic knife: FarmVille imitates a **closed classical market** (buy input, sell output, no soil limit) rather than Daly-style ecological economics. Cute fields, no waste, no carrying capacity. That is the model not to copy for Stalk.

DiGRA on FarmVille 2 adds the social clause: virtual goods only hold *meaning* inside a real neighbor graph. A stall with no friends is just a shop.

So FarmVille supports: time-gated plant, coin profit on the cheap crop, extra drops as *social* surplus, neighbor caps. It does **not** support seed-as-commodity if we have already promised zero-grain Stalk.

## What SimCity actually did

Agriculture is not a money printer. It is a **phase of a small city**.

- IA demand cap starts around 30,000 and is **eaten by every other job**. More offices and factories → fewer farms. There is no building that raises the farm cap.
- Farms want low land value, low traffic, low air pollution, flat land, short freight trips. They pollute water. They employ poor-wealth labor. They vanish as the skyline rises.
- Industry in general only raises its cap when freight **leaves** the city (neighbor deal / port). Production that cannot export hits a wall.
- The closed loops that *do* exist are sanitation: recycling knocks garbage down ~20%; waste-to-energy turns trash into power — a sink that becomes a civic good.

SimCity’s lesson for seeds: the basic farm stays viable in a quiet town; it does not scale 1:1 with population. Extra plots are expansion, taxed by land value and grain. Export (stamps, later GE) is how surplus leaves without poisoning the residential board.

## What academia actually supports

**Ostrom, not Hardin.** Common-pool resources survive when the group has clear boundaries, rules that fit local conditions, monitoring, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict paths, and nested layers — not when a central bank prints or a free-for-all harvests. A 2023 Catan experiment crashed the shared bank every time those principles were off, and held when they were on. A 2025 PNAS evolutionary-game paper shows harvest norms can self-organize if players can see stock and punish over-take.

Map that onto the porch:

| Ostrom principle | PlankSpace |
|---|---|
| Boundaries | Wallet owns the board; six tilled plots are the CPR |
| Congruence | Time + one seed in, faces out; seed returns |
| Collective choice | Ritual birth of new faces later, not admin mint |
| Monitoring | Signed receipts, visible glut |
| Graduated sanctions | \(S/D\) shrinks yield; wash trades ignored |
| Nested | Porch → Lumberyard → downtown `$PLANK` |

**Faucet–drain, not a closed pipe.** Ultima Online tied drain back into the faucet. Hoarding froze the world; they had to print. Castronova’s three pillars for virtual economies: stable unit of account, rational prices, distribution that does not strand newcomers. P2E papers in 2025 say the same in uglier clothes: no sink, no survival.

So: **grain** is faucet–drain (play in, stamps/expansions/advanced plants out). **Stalk seeds** are a recycled tool, almost a closed loop on the six plots. **Stalk faces** are faucet–drain (harvest in, stamp/socket/gift/later list out). Do not pipe faces back into seeds.

**Permaculture / doughnut / circular economy.** The useful overlap is: save seed, spend fruit, keep a social floor and an ecological ceiling. Community seed banks in the development literature run on save–exchange–sell-a-little, not on repurchase from four petrochemical firms. Stardew’s Seed Maker is the game version of that — fruit *may* become extra seed, with loss — which is how you grow the tin without a 1:1+ printer.

**Do not** treat “positive-sum” as “everyone’s stack goes up forever.” Positive-sum here means more *boards* and more *use*, not more units per click. Chang and the FarmVille 3 “cute but dark” paper are warnings: cute loops that hide extraction are still extraction.

## The lifecycle, locked

```
plant Stalk on a tilled plot     → consume 1 stalk_seed, 0 grain
successful harvest               → +H stalk faces  AND  +1 stalk_seed
miss / compost                   → +0 faces, +1 stalk_seed
stamp / socket / sunk gift       → −faces, seed untouched
```

On a stable six-plot Stalk farm, seed count stays about six. Faces accumulate only if you refuse to talk. That is cheap talk: the crop remakes the means; the speech spends the surplus.

**More seeds** are a different ladder, FarmVille-bushel-shaped and Ostrom-capped:

- small chance of a +1 extra seed on harvest (mastery / loved board), never a second guaranteed seed
- neighbor gift, hard daily cap
- expansion cells cost grain + land value, and need a seed you actually have

Do not let harvest return two seeds. That is a printer. Do not refuse the seed on success. That is a shop, and it breaks zero-grain Stalk.

Splinter and the rest stay seed **+ grain** on plant. Their harvest can return **their own** seed the same way, still one-for-one. Grain paid on plant is the drain that keeps rare faces from becoming a second Stalk.

SimCity’s agriculture cap is already in the land-value formula: as the city fills with unused faces, soil tightens. FarmVille’s strawberry is already in the clock. Ostrom is already in the six-plot boundary and the signed receipt. Permaculture is the seed that comes home.

Stalk remakes Stalk’s seed. Talk spends Stalk. That is the sustainable loop.
